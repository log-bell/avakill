# Founder's Weekly Schedule

## Reality check

You have a 5-month-old, a wife who works 8-5, an enterprise client expecting 10 hours/week, an open-source product to launch, a SaaS platform to build, and your own health to maintain. This schedule is designed around one principle: **protect the non-negotiables first, then fill the gaps with work.**

Non-negotiables (in order):
1. Baby and family
2. Health (gym 3x, running 3x)
3. Enterprise client (10 hrs/week — this pays the bills)
4. AvaKill platform + growth

---

## Time budget (weekly)

| Category | Hours/week | When |
|----------|-----------|------|
| **Deep work (uninterrupted)** | 15 hrs | 5:00-8:00 AM (M-F) = 15 hrs |
| **Baby-compatible work** | 25 hrs | 8:00 AM-5:00 PM with interruptions (realistic: ~5 hrs/day usable) |
| **Sunday afternoon** | 3-4 hrs | After church, optional |
| **Total working hours** | ~40-44 hrs | |
| **Enterprise client** | 10 hrs | Carved from the above |
| **AvaKill (platform + growth)** | 30-34 hrs | Everything else |

### The honest math on baby-compatible work

A 5-month-old naps 2-3 times/day for 30-90 minutes each. Between naps: feeding, changing, play, fussiness. Realistically, you get **fragmented 20-45 minute windows** during the day. Your wife's three visits (two 10-min feeds + one hour lunch) give you slightly longer blocks. Plan accordingly:

- **Deep thinking / architecture / writing code** → morning block only (5-8 AM)
- **Meetings, email, social media, reviews, admin** → baby-compatible (daytime)
- **AI-assisted coding sessions** → baby-compatible (Claude does the heavy lifting, you review in fragments)

| Day | Time | Activity |

| Friday | 7:15-9:00 AM | Run club (with baby) |

Friday is a double session (gym + run club). That's 4 runs and 3 gym sessions per week — above your 3/3 target. The run club is also social time and gets your son out of the house, which is a win on multiple levels. If Friday's double feels like too much some weeks, skip the solo run on Thursday instead.

The 5 AM slot works because your wife is still home. If the baby had a rough night and you're running on 4 hours of sleep, skip the workout — sleep is more important than any single session. Consistency over intensity. Missing one day doesn't matter. Missing a week does.

## What goes where: task-type routing

### Deep work blocks (5-8 AM, quiet, uninterrupted)
- Architecture decisions
- Writing new code from scratch
- Complex debugging
- Database schema design
- Writing (blog posts, documentation, HN post drafts)
- Enterprise client's core agent development

### Baby-compatible blocks (8 AM-5 PM, fragmented)
- AI-assisted coding (start a Claude session, review outputs between baby tasks)
- Code reviews and PR merges
- Email and async communication
- Social media engagement (5-10 min bursts)
- Reading research, competitor analysis
- Meetings (schedule during nap windows or wife's visits)
- DevOps, CI/CD, deployment tasks
- Testing and QA
- Administrative (invoicing, planning, notes)

### Friday afternoon (weekly ritual)
- Review what shipped this week
- Update task board / project tracker
- Plan next week's deep work priorities (write them down Sunday night or Monday morning)
- Batch-schedule social media for the following week
- Check metrics: GitHub stars, installs, website traffic, revenue

## Weekly priorities framework

Each week should advance **three tracks**. Write down your top priority for each on Sunday night or Friday afternoon:

1. **Enterprise client** — What's the one deliverable this week?
2. **AvaKill platform** — What's the one feature/milestone this week?
3. **AvaKill growth** — What's the one outreach/content action this week?

If you only get those three things done, the week was successful. Everything else is bonus.

## Sustainability rules

1. **Sleep is sacred.** A 5 AM alarm means a 9:30 PM bedtime. Protect this. You cannot code, parent, or think on chronic sleep deprivation.
2. **Saturday is off.** No exceptions. If you find yourself "just checking one thing," you're burning out.
3. **The baby comes first.** If he's crying, close the laptop. No PR is more important. The code will be there in 20 minutes. Your son won't be 5 months old again.
4. **Eat real food.** You're working out 6x/week and running on fragmented sleep. Meal prep on Sunday or get meals sorted. Skipping lunch to code is a false economy.
5. **Weekly date night or quality time with your wife.** Even 30 minutes after the baby is down. She's carrying half this load and working full-time. Don't let the startup eat the marriage.
6. **One bad day doesn't break the system.** Baby was up all night? Skip the 5 AM workout, start work at 8, do what you can. Reset tomorrow. The schedule is a guide, not a cage.

## When the enterprise client schedule solidifies

Once you know their preferred meeting times and delivery cadence, update this doc:

- [ ] Anchor client meetings to Monday or Wednesday (ideally during wife's lunch hour)
- [ ] Define weekly deliverable format (async Loom update? Written summary? Live demo?)
- [ ] Set up a shared project board so they have visibility without needing live meetings
- [ ] Block "client prep" time (30 min before any meeting) in the schedule

## Quarterly check-in questions

Every 3 months, review this schedule honestly:

1. Am I actually hitting 5 AM, or is it aspirational?
2. Is the baby-compatible work actually productive, or am I kidding myself?
3. Am I protecting Saturday?
4. Is the enterprise client taking more than 10 hours? (If yes, renegotiate or raise rates.)
5. Am I making progress on AvaKill platform, or is it always getting squeezed out by client work?
6. How's my wife feeling about the balance? Ask her directly.
